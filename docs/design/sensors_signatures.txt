SENSOR AND SIGNATURE SYSTEM — PHASE 1 SPEC

OVERVIEW

Ships can be detected through several different signatures produced by their mass, energy use, electronic activity, and computational activity.

The Phase 1 signature model uses four signature types:

Thermal
Gravitational
Electromagnetic
Computational

Sensors are equipment modules that detect one or more of these signatures.

Detection and target locking are related but distinct.

Detection establishes that a contact exists and provides some information about it.

Target lock establishes a sufficiently reliable track to support weapons targeting and other combat functions.

SIGNATURE TYPES

THERMAL SIGNATURE

Thermal signature represents heat emitted by the ship and its systems.

Sources include:

Power plants
Engines
Life support
Weapons
Other active ship systems
Waste heat

Thermal signature generally increases with ship activity and power expenditure.

A ship operating at high output is therefore easier to detect thermally.

A ship operating at low power can reduce its thermal signature, although it cannot eliminate waste heat completely.

GRAVITATIONAL SIGNATURE

Gravitational signature represents the detectable gravitational effect of the ship and its mass.

It is fundamentally different from an emitted signal: the ship does not need to be actively transmitting anything to produce it.

Large ships should generally produce stronger gravitational signatures than small ships.

Potential sources of variation include:

Ship mass
Distance
Local gravitational environment
Advanced propulsion or other high-energy systems

A gravitational sensor therefore provides a way to detect relatively passive ships that are attempting to minimise other signatures.

ELECTROMAGNETIC SIGNATURE

Electromagnetic signature represents electromagnetic emissions generated by the ship.

Sources include:

Power generation
Electrical systems
Computers
Communications
Active electronics
Other powered equipment

An actively transmitting or heavily operating ship produces a stronger electromagnetic signature.

A ship can reduce its electromagnetic signature by reducing unnecessary emissions and powered systems.

COMPUTATIONAL SIGNATURE

Computational signature represents detectable activity associated with the ship's computing systems.

Sources include:

Sensor processing
Navigation
Target tracking
Fire control
Cyber operations
Other computational workloads

A ship using substantial compute capacity therefore produces a stronger computational signature.

This directly connects the signature model to the ship's Compute resource.

A ship can reduce its computational signature by shutting down or reducing non-essential computational workloads.

SENSOR TYPES

Sensors are specialised according to the signatures they are designed to detect.

THERMAL SCANNER

Primary signature:

Thermal

Purpose:

Detect and track ships through their thermal emissions.

Strengths:

Good detection of powered or maneuvering ships
Useful for tracking ships operating at high output
Can identify strong engine or reactor activity

Weaknesses:

Low-power ships are harder to detect
Background heat sources can create interference
Ships can reduce their signature by reducing activity

GRAVIMETRIC SCANNER

Primary signature:

Gravitational

Purpose:

Detect ships through their gravitational signature.

Strengths:

Does not depend on active emissions
Useful against ships attempting to minimise other signatures
Particularly useful for detecting larger vessels

Weaknesses:

Small ships produce weaker signatures
Natural gravitational sources can create background noise
Detection precision decreases with distance

EM SPECTRUM SCANNER

Primary signature:

Electromagnetic

Purpose:

Detect electromagnetic emissions from ships and their systems.

Strengths:

Detects active ships and electronic systems
Can detect communications
Useful for identifying powered activity
Can support target tracking

Weaknesses:

Passive ships may be difficult to detect
Deliberate emissions make the source easier to detect
Electromagnetic background sources can create clutter

COMPUTATIONAL SCANNER

Primary signature:

Computational

Purpose:

Detect computational activity associated with other ships.

Strengths:

Detects ships operating substantial computational workloads
Can reveal active sensor processing
Can reveal targeting/fire-control activity
Potentially useful for detecting cyber activity
Provides an alternative to conventional physical signatures

Weaknesses:

Low-compute ships are harder to detect
Ships can reduce their signature by reducing computational activity
Different computing technologies may produce different signatures

GENERAL-PURPOSE SENSOR SUITE

Primary signatures:

Thermal
Gravitational
Electromagnetic
Computational

Purpose:

General-purpose detection and tracking.

A basic sensor suite has moderate sensitivity across multiple signature types rather than specialising in one.

This represents the standard sensor equipment for ordinary ships.

ADVANCED SENSOR SUITE

Primary signatures:

Thermal
Gravitational
Electromagnetic
Computational

Purpose:

Higher-performance detection, tracking and contact resolution.

An advanced sensor suite has greater sensitivity and/or range than a basic sensor suite.

It can also combine information from multiple signature channels to produce a more reliable contact.

The existing prototype already distinguishes sensor_basic and sensor_advanced, with the advanced sensor providing extended-range sensing. This signature system provides a more detailed underlying model for those existing equipment levels.

DETECTION

A sensor attempts to detect signatures within its effective sensing range.

A successful detection produces a contact.

The contact can initially contain limited information, for example:

Unknown contact

Weak thermal signature

The sensor does not necessarily know the exact identity, ship type, or configuration of the contact immediately.

Different signatures can provide different levels of information.

MULTI-SIGNATURE DETECTION

A ship can produce several signatures simultaneously.

For example, a ship operating normally might produce:

Thermal: Medium
Gravitational: Low
Electromagnetic: Medium
Computational: High

A sensor that detects several of these can correlate them into a more reliable contact.

A specialised sensor may detect a contact that another sensor misses.

SIGNATURE MANAGEMENT

Ships can deliberately reduce their signatures by changing how they operate.

Examples:

Reducing power generation can reduce thermal and electromagnetic emissions.

Reducing computational activity can reduce computational signature.

Reducing communications and active electronics can reduce electromagnetic signature.

Reducing propulsion activity can reduce propulsion-related activity within the thermal and electromagnetic signatures.

Gravitational signature is comparatively difficult to suppress because it is fundamentally associated with the ship itself.

This creates a distinction between:

ACTIVE SIGNATURES

Signatures that can be substantially affected by ship operation.

Thermal
Electromagnetic
Computational

PASSIVE SIGNATURE

A signature that fundamentally results from the ship's physical presence.

Gravitational

TARGET LOCK

Detection does not automatically produce a target lock.

A target lock requires a sufficiently strong and reliable track.

Factors affecting target lock can include:

Signature strength
Sensor sensitivity
Distance
Sensor quality
Contact stability
Background interference
Number of signatures being correlated
Target activity

A contact may therefore be detected without being lockable.

For example:

"Unknown contact — weak thermal signature"

means the sensor knows something is present.

"Target lock acquired"

means the sensor has established a sufficiently reliable track for targeting.

TARGETING VALUE OF SIGNATURES

Different signatures can provide different tactical advantages.

Thermal:

Good for finding ships that are actively operating.

Gravitational:

Good for finding ships that cannot completely hide their physical presence.

Electromagnetic:

Good for detecting powered and transmitting ships.

Computational:

Good for detecting ships actively using sophisticated onboard systems.

This means a ship can attempt to remain difficult to detect by changing its operating profile rather than simply equipping a generic "stealth module."

SENSOR AND COMPUTE

Sensors themselves consume ship resources.

The existing equipment model treats sensors as system modules, while ship systems generally operate within Power and Compute budgets.

An advanced sensor therefore does not have to be strictly superior in every circumstance.

A powerful sensor could consume substantially more Power and/or Compute.

A ship attempting to operate quietly might deliberately run its sensors at reduced capability, reducing its own Computational and Electromagnetic signatures while sacrificing detection performance.

CORE PHASE 1 PRINCIPLE

Sensors answer:

"What can I detect?"

Signatures answer:

"What does the target reveal about itself?"

Target lock answers:

"Do I have a sufficiently reliable track to target it?"

The four signature channels are:

THERMAL
GRAVITATIONAL
ELECTROMAGNETIC
COMPUTATIONAL

This gives the sensor system a direct relationship with the wider ship model without requiring a separate stealth statistic.

A ship's configuration, activity and resource usage determine what it reveals.

A sensor's capabilities determine what it can perceive.

A target lock represents the point at which the sensor information becomes sufficiently reliable for combat targeting.
