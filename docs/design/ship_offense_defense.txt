PHASE 1 SHIP COMBAT — OFFENSIVE & DEFENSIVE SYSTEM SPEC

CORE MODEL

Ships have three primary resources that can be degraded in combat:

Hits
Power
Compute

Hits represent physical hull integrity.

Power represents available shipboard power generation. Installed systems consume power, so reducing available power can force systems offline.

Compute represents available computational capacity. Installed systems consume compute cycles, so reducing compute can disable or degrade computational systems.

Weapons do not directly specify which resource they damage. Instead, weapons generate damage packets. Damage packets are processed against the target's installed defensive systems and ultimately translated into resource damage or system disruption.


DAMAGE PACKETS

Phase 1 uses four damage packet types:

Kinetic
Concussive
Energy
Cyber

KINETIC

Physical momentum delivered by a projectile or other solid object.

Typical sources:
- Mass drivers
- Conventional projectile weapons
- Missile kinetic components
- Fragments / shrapnel

Primary effect:
- Hull damage
- Armour interaction

Typical secondary effect:
- Limited physical system damage

Example packet:
Kinetic 40


CONCUSSIVE

Mechanical shock, explosive, blast and structural disruption.

Typical sources:
- Explosive warheads
- Plasma weapons
- Scatter weapons
- Heavy impacts

Primary effect:
- Hull damage
- Physical system disruption

Typical secondary effect:
- Greater tendency than Kinetic to disrupt installed machinery

Example packet:
Concussive 30


ENERGY

Directed or concentrated energy delivered to the target.

Typical sources:
- Lasers
- Turbo lasers
- Plasma weapons
- Other directed-energy weapons

Primary effect:
- Hull damage
- System damage

Example packet:
Energy 25


CYBER

Attack against the computational and electronic systems of the target ship.

Typical sources:
- Computer intrusion
- Electronic warfare
- EMP attacks

Primary effect:
- Compute degradation
- Disablement of compute-dependent systems

Example packet:
Cyber 20

DAMAGE PACKETS AND DELIVERY SYSTEMS

Damage type and delivery type are separate concepts.

Damage packet:
What the attack does.

Delivery type:
How the attack reaches the target.

A weapon can therefore produce one or several damage packets.

Examples:

Light Mass Driver
Delivery: Ballistic
Packet: Kinetic

Rocket
Delivery: Ballistic
Packet: Kinetic + Concussive

Guided Missile / Drone
Delivery: Guided
Packet: Kinetic + Concussive

Laser
Delivery: Beam
Packet: Energy

Plasma Cannon
Delivery: Plasma
Packet: Energy + Concussive

Networked Intrusion
Delivery: Cyber
Packet: Cyber

EMP Missile
Delivery: Guided
Packet: Cyber + Concussive


OFFENSIVE DELIVERY TYPES

Phase 1 uses five basic delivery types:

Ballistic
Beam
Plasma
Guided
Cyber


BALLISTIC

A physical projectile travels from the attacking vessel to the target.

Examples:
- Mass driver
- Conventional cannon
- Projectile weapon

Typical damage:
- Kinetic
- Kinetic + Concussive

Defended against primarily by:
- Armour
- Point defence


BEAM

Energy is directed at the target without a conventional projectile travelling to it.

Examples:
- Laser
- Turbo laser

Typical damage:
- Energy

Defended against primarily by:
- Shields
- Energy protection


PLASMA

A high-energy plasma attack is delivered toward the target.

Examples:
- Plasma cannon
- Plasma burst cannon

Typical damage:
- Energy
- Energy + Concussive

Plasma is retained as a separate delivery type in Phase 1 because it is an established weapon family in the existing equipment design and can have distinct defensive interactions from conventional beam weapons.

Defended against primarily by:
- Shields
- Energy protection
- Armour after shield penetration


GUIDED

A physical munition travels independently toward the target.

Examples:
- Missiles
- Drones

Typical damage:
- Kinetic
- Concussive
- Kinetic + Concussive
- Other warhead combinations later

Defended against primarily by:
- Point defence
- Missile countermeasures
- Shields, depending on shield design


CYBER

An attack is delivered through the target's electronic and computational environment.

Examples:
- Intrusion attack
- Electronic warfare

Typical damage:
- Cyber

Defended against primarily by:
- Cyber defence
- Defensive computing
- Other electronic countermeasures


WEAPON PROPERTIES

The delivery type and damage packet are not intended to describe every characteristic of a weapon.

Other properties remain separate:

- Range
- Rate of fire
- Accuracy
- Tracking
- Ammunition requirement
- Magazine capacity
- Projectile/munition speed
- Area of effect
- Guidance quality
- Armour penetration
- Shield interaction
- Power demand
- Compute demand

These properties modify how a weapon performs without becoming additional damage types.

For example, "fragmentation" is a weapon characteristic rather than a damage type.

"Penetrating" is a weapon characteristic rather than a damage type.

"Area effect" is a weapon characteristic rather than a damage type.

DEFENSIVE SYSTEMS

Phase 1 defensive equipment consists of:

Armour
Shields
Point Defence
Cyber Defence


ARMOUR

Passive physical protection.

Armour reduces incoming physical damage before it is applied to the ship's underlying hull and systems.

Primary interaction:
- Kinetic
- Concussive

Secondary interaction:
- Energy, depending on armour technology

Armour is represented as an installed module and contributes additional effective hull protection.

The existing equipment model already uses Chitanium armour modules that add Hits to the assembled ship.


SHIELDS

Shields are active defensive systems.

A shield does not simply have a single "shield strength" against everything. Each shield has a protection profile describing how strongly it protects against each damage packet type.

Example:

Deflector Shield
Kinetic: high
Concussive: high
Energy: low
Cyber: none

Energy Shield
Kinetic: low
Concussive: medium
Energy: high
Cyber: none

Electronic Shield
Kinetic: none
Concussive: none
Energy: low
Cyber: high

SHIELD PROFILE

A shield therefore has at least:

- Shield capacity
- Protection against Kinetic
- Protection against Concussive
- Protection against Energy
- Protection against Cyber
- Power demand
- Mass
- Volume
- Regeneration/recovery characteristics

The protection values determine how much of an incoming damage packet is absorbed or mitigated.

For example:

Incoming:
Energy 50

Shield:
Energy protection 60%

Result:
20 Energy reaches the ship.


SHIELD SPECIALISATION

Shield designs should generally involve tradeoffs.

A cheap specialist shield might provide very high protection against one damage channel while providing little protection against others.

A general-purpose shield might provide moderate protection across several channels.

An expensive military shield might provide strong protection across several channels but consume substantial power.

This allows shield selection to become part of ship fitting without requiring a huge number of separate shield mechanics.


POINT DEFENCE

Point defence protects the ship from physical incoming threats before they reach the ship.

Primary targets:
- Ballistic projectiles
- Guided missiles
- Other interceptable physical threats

Point defence is not a conventional shield.

It attacks or intercepts the incoming weapon rather than reducing the damage packet after impact.

Point defence therefore introduces a separate interception stage:

Incoming weapon
→ Point defence attempt
→ If intercepted: no damage
→ If not intercepted: damage packet proceeds to shields/armour


CYBER DEFENCE

Cyber defence protects the ship's computational systems against Cyber attacks.

A successful Cyber attack can reduce effective Compute Capacity.

Cyber defence may:
- Block an attack
- Reduce Cyber damage
- Detect an intrusion
- Prevent specific systems from being compromised

Cyber defence itself consumes ship resources, particularly Compute and potentially Power.

This creates a deliberate tradeoff:

More defensive computing makes a ship harder to compromise, but consumes some of the same computational capacity that could otherwise support offensive and utility systems.


COMBAT RESOLUTION

A basic Phase 1 attack follows this sequence:

1. Weapon fires.

2. Weapon's delivery type determines the relevant interception/defence stage.

3. Point defence or other delivery-specific defence attempts to defeat the attack where applicable.

4. If the attack reaches the target, its damage packets are generated.

5. Shields process the incoming packets according to their protection profile.

6. Armour processes remaining physical damage.

7. Remaining damage is applied to the appropriate ship resources.

8. Resulting Power and Compute reductions can disable installed systems if the ship can no longer meet their operating requirements.


EXAMPLE: MASS DRIVER

Attacker:
Light Mass Driver

Delivery:
Ballistic

Packet:
Kinetic 40

Defence:
Point defence fails.

Shield:
Deflector absorbs 25%.

Remaining:
Kinetic 30

Armour:
Reduces the effective physical damage.

Remaining damage:
Applied to Hits.


EXAMPLE: MISSILE

Attacker:
Missile Launcher

Delivery:
Guided

Packet:
Kinetic 25
Concussive 35

Defence:
Point defence attempts interception.

If interception fails:

Shield processes both packets according to its Kinetic and Concussive protection.

Armour then processes the remaining physical damage.

Remaining damage is applied to the ship.


EXAMPLE: LASER

Attacker:
Laser

Delivery:
Beam

Packet:
Energy 30

There is no projectile for point defence to intercept.

Shield processes Energy damage.

Remaining Energy damage reaches armour/hull.


EXAMPLE: CYBER ATTACK

Attacker:
Cyber Intrusion Suite

Delivery:
Cyber

Packet:
Cyber 30

Conventional armour:
Irrelevant.

Conventional point defence:
Irrelevant.

Cyber defence:
Attempts to block or reduce the attack.

Remaining Cyber damage reduces effective Compute Capacity.

If Compute Capacity falls below the demand of installed systems, those systems become unavailable.


RESOURCE DEGRADATION

The important Phase 1 design principle is that combat does not simply reduce Hits.

A ship can be defeated or seriously crippled in several ways.

Hull degradation:
Hits fall.

Power degradation:
Available MW falls.

Compute degradation:
Available CU falls.

A ship might therefore survive physically while becoming incapable of operating its weapons, sensors, life support, propulsion, or other systems.

For example:

Ship has:
60 MW generation
45 MW installed demand

An attack reduces effective power generation to:
30 MW

The ship is now operating with a 15 MW deficit.

Systems must shut down or become unavailable until demand falls below available generation.


Likewise:

Ship has:
40 CU
35 CU installed demand

Cyber attack reduces effective Compute Capacity to:
20 CU

The ship can no longer operate all of its compute-dependent systems.

This means offensive systems can have fundamentally different tactical purposes without requiring artificial "damage to CPU" weapons.

PHASE 1 IMPLEMENTATION NOTES (locked for Godot build)

Packet-to-resource conversion (after shields and armour):
  Kinetic    -> Hits 1.0,  Power 0.10, Compute 0.0
  Concussive -> Hits 0.8,  Power 0.25, Compute 0.0
  Energy     -> Hits 0.6,  Power 0.20, Compute 0.10
  Cyber      -> Hits 0.0,  Power 0.0,  Compute 1.0

Scatter weapons use delivery Ballistic; area_effect is a weapon flag, not a delivery type.

Warheads: ammunition supplies damage_packets when the weapon consumes ammo; otherwise
the weapon module carries damage_packets (lasers, cyber suite).

Rocket vs guided missile: rocket launcher is Ballistic delivery; guided missile launcher
is Guided delivery. Both may share HE warheads; EMP rockets carry Cyber-only packets.
Ballistic/guided EMP is interceptable by point defence; remaining Cyber ignores armour
and kinetic/concussive/energy shields but is reduced by electronic shields and cyber defence.

Plasma delivery is explicit on the weapon module (never inferred from ammunition type).
Point defence does not intercept beam, plasma, or cyber delivery.

Armour adds Hits and provides a protection profile vs kinetic/concussive/energy; cyber
ignores armour.

Existing catalog module ids (light_laser, light_mass_driver, missile_launcher, etc.) are
preserved; missile_launcher display name is Rocket Launcher (unguided).
